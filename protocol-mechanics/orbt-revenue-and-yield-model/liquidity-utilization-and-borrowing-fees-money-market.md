# Liquidity Utilization & Borrowing Fees (Money Market)

### **Facilitator Fees and Profit-Sharing**

Facilitators pay the protocol via **flat fees** and a **share of profits**. The flat fees, often paid monthly in **0xUSD**, provide a steady income stream — similar to a subscription or licensing model. This ensures consistent protocol revenue, even during periods of lower market activity.

Profit-sharing, on the other hand, is **performance-based** and can be extremely lucrative in favorable conditions. For example, if a facilitator generates **$5M profit** in a quarter and the profit-share rate is **20%**, ORBT earns **$1M** from that facilitator. With multiple facilitators, this can quickly accumulate into a significant revenue stream.

Because facilitators often manage **large volumes** and **complex opportunities**, this income is closely tied to **overall market activity**. During volatile markets with frequent arbitrage or liquidity gaps, ORBT can experience strong revenue growth as facilitators capture and share profits.

This model also **incentivizes ORBT to onboard and support skilled facilitators**, much like a platform that empowers its top “vendors” to thrive while earning a commission on their success. It creates a mutually reinforcing relationship — facilitators grow their earnings, and ORBT benefits from their performance.

### **Yield from Strategic Liquidity Deployment:**

ORBT’s own capital — including **protocol-owned assets**, **treasury reserves**, and other internal funds — is strategically deployed to earn yield. For instance, the protocol may allocate portions of **UCE reserves** (such as excess USDC beyond the 25% minimum liquidity threshold) into **low-risk yield opportunities** like lending on **Aave** or purchasing **short-term treasury bills** through **RWA integrations**.

The **ORBT Money Market** can also deploy unutilized liquidity externally or through partnerships, generating additional returns. All interest and gains from these deployments flow directly back to the protocol.

According to the **FactBlock report**, ORBT follows a design where **75% of assets** are allocated to **strategies and yield opportunities**, while **25%** remain as **instant liquidity**. This ensures that a significant portion of the protocol’s reserves are continuously working in **yield-bearing positions**.

Some of this yield is distributed to users, particularly when derived from their own deposited funds. However, the protocol also retains a **performance share**, similar to how hedge funds charge management or performance fees.

Additionally, ORBT can act as a **market maker** or **liquidity provider** using its own capital, earning trading fees that accrue to the **treasury**.

In this way, ORBT operates as a **DeFi investor**, leveraging its complete visibility across Pockets and capital flows to **optimize treasury deployment** and maintain a stable **baseline yield** — for example, targeting safe stablecoin yields that can help sustain savings rate payouts.
