# Governance-Driven Buyback & Burn Mechanics

Though not explicitly listed, ORBT may also generate revenue from **secondary and governance-controlled sources**. These include:

* **Liquidation fees:** When a user’s collateral is liquidated, a small penalty fee (similar to MakerDAO’s model) may be charged, with a portion directed to ORBT’s surplus.
* **Oracle or data services:** If ORBT develops proprietary **pricing feeds**, **bridges**, or **market data tools**, access to these could be monetized for external users, though this remains speculative.
* **Governance-branded services:** ORBT could eventually launch **front-end interfaces or aggregators** that charge small fees routed directly to the DAO treasury.
* **Savings rate spreads:** If real-world asset (RWA) yield is **10%** and users are offered **8%**, the remaining **2%** spread could accrue to the protocol as revenue.

Over time, ORBT’s governance may allocate a portion of these **surplus revenues** toward **buyback and burn mechanisms** — using treasury funds to repurchase ORBT tokens from the market, then **burn** them or **redistribute** them to stakers through the **USM**.

These governance-directed buybacks strengthen **token scarcity**, **align long-term holder value** with protocol growth, and **recycle treasury surplus** into sustainable incentives that reinforce ecosystem health.
