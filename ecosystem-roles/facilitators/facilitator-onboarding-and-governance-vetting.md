# Facilitator Onboarding & Governance Vetting

Prospective facilitators begin by submitting an **application or proposal** to the **ORBT DAO**. Each submission includes detailed information about their **proposed strategy**, **technical capabilities**, **past performance**, **risk management framework**, and **requested operational terms** such as fee share percentages or minting capacity.

The proposal undergoes a **governance review process**, where ORBT token holders — and, if established, a designated **Facilitator Committee** — evaluate the submission. Governance may assess parameters such as strategic value, historical reliability, security posture, and potential protocol impact.

Following this review, the proposal is put to a **governance vote** for approval or rejection. The process mirrors **Aave’s GHO facilitator framework**, where facilitators are approved with predefined **minting or liquidity caps**.

Once approved, a facilitator is granted an official **Facilitator Role** within the ORBT protocol’s smart contracts. Alongside this role, they receive defined **operational limits** that determine how much of each **0xAsset** they are authorized to mint, deploy, or manage. These constraints typically include:

* **Bucket capacity:** the maximum allowable amount of 0xAssets that can be minted or managed.
* **Pocket limits:** the maximum number of concurrent strategies or Pockets that can be active.
* **Volume constraints:** daily or per-transaction limits for liquidity deployment.

For example, a facilitator approved for an arbitrage strategy might be authorized to mint up to **10 million 0xUSD** for deployment into specific Pockets. These constraints ensure that no single facilitator can overextend system resources or introduce undue risk, maintaining overall protocol stability and balance.
