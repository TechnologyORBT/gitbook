# Disclaimers of Liability and Forward-Looking Statements

No Liability: The Terms will explicitly state that ORBT developers, Foundation, and DAO members are not liable for any damages: direct, indirect, incidental, or consequential. This covers scenarios like smart contract failures, hacks, or even mistakes in the whitepaper. “The protocol is experimental” etc. Since ORBT is decentralized, there’s arguably no single party to hold accountable, but this is spelled out: users cannot claim compensation from ORBT for losses.

Force Majeure: Standard clause that if external events (network outages, force majeure events, regulatory crackdown, war, etc.) cause issues, the protocol’s operators aren’t responsible for inability to operate or losses.

Forward-Looking Statements: The whitepaper might contain projections or language like “expects”, “anticipates”. A disclaimer similar to those in business documents might be included: “This document contains forward-looking statements that are subject to risks and uncertainties. Actual results may differ materially from those contemplated by these statements.” It basically guards that anything said about future features, targets, roadmap can change (e.g., “While we plan to incorporate real-world assets, regulatory hurdles might delay or change these plans”).

Not an Offering Document: If ORBT token distribution happens (or happened) separate from this paper, the whitepaper clarifies it is not a prospectus or offering memorandum. It’s technical and conceptual in nature, and not meant to induce investment. There might have been separate terms for any token sale (if one occurred), which this doesn’t override.

Open-Source and No Warranty: The code is provided “as is” without warranty of any kind. No guarantee that it’s bug-free or fit for any specific purpose. Usually open source licenses include such disclaimers; the whitepaper can reiterate that.

Community Governance Autonomy: A note that once the DAO is in control, decisions are made by token holders, and the initial team might not have unilateral control. So any statements in this document are subject to future governance changes. E.g., “Protocol parameters described (like fee percentages, etc.) could be altered by ORBT governance in the future. This document reflects the system at launch, but the community may evolve it.” So no one can claim the team “promised” X forever because the DAO could change it with due process.

Privacy: There is typically a note that while ORBT doesn’t collect personal data beyond maybe web analytics, any data provided by users (like if one uses a contact form or KYC for facilitator roles) is handled per a Privacy Policy. But on-chain, transactions are pseudo-anonymous not truly private; users should not expect privacy on their financial transactions beyond what Ethereum pseudonymity provides.

Conclusion of Disclaimers: The final tone is that ORBT is an innovative financial infrastructure with risks. Users and developers engaging with it acknowledge these risks and responsibilities. The project is committed to transparency and best practices, but it cannot eliminate risk. All participants should consider their actions carefully.
