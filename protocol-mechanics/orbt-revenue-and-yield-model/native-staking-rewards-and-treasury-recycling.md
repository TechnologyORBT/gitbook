# Native Staking Rewards & Treasury Recycling

While staking is typically viewed as a **rewards distribution mechanism** (an outflow), it can also indirectly serve as a **revenue contributor and value stabilizer** for the protocol.

For example, if ORBT requires certain actions — such as **becoming a facilitator** or **accessing higher yield tiers** — to involve staking **ORBT tokens**, that locks value within the system and may include associated fees.

More concretely, the **User Staking Module (USM)** can impose **early withdrawal penalties** or **performance bonds**. In some cases, if a staker withdraws before a defined lock period, a small fee is charged — directed either to the **treasury** or redistributed to other stakers. Similarly, if facilitators are required to stake ORBT tokens as **insurance**, and they default, those tokens are **seized**, creating an effective gain for the protocol (though this primarily serves as **risk mitigation**, not planned revenue).

The reference to “native staking via USM” in revenue flows may also extend to **protocol utility functions** — for example, small governance participation fees or anti-spam burns when submitting proposals, similar to mechanisms used in other DAO frameworks.

At a broader level, the protocol benefits because not all rewards are distributed. Some are **retained**, strengthening the **treasury**, which in turn **supports future staking rewards**.

In essence, staking encourages **long-term holding of ORBT**, which can increase its token value and amplify the protocol’s **buyback capacity**. This creates a **positive feedback loop** — staking drives value retention, which supports revenue, which further rewards stakers.

The **USM** thus aligns stakers’ incentives with overall **protocol growth**:

* Stakers share in revenue,
* Unclaimed or forfeited rewards revert to the treasury, and
* Stakers help secure the system, preventing losses that would otherwise reduce protocol income.
