# Regulatory Compliance and Decentralization

ORBT DAO Foundation: FactBlock’s partnership piece suggests ORBT DAO Foundation is established, possibly in a crypto-friendly jurisdiction (e.g., Switzerland, Cayman, Singapore). This foundation likely handles initial coordination and perhaps legal stewardship of the project (like holding IP, paying for audits, etc.). Over time, ORBT governance aims to fully decentralize operations. The whitepaper might mention this entity just to reassure that some formal organization exists to interface with regulators if needed, and to sponsor development.

Securities Law and ORBT Token: A crucial disclaimer is that ORBT token is a governance token, not a share of a company. The whitepaper (and any token sale docs) emphasize its utility nature: governance, fee discounts, etc., and that any appreciation is a function of community adoption, not promises of profit by a centralized entity. However, given the buyback mechanism, one might worry regulators see it as akin to a dividend. ORBT likely tries to follow the path of Maker or Aave where governance tokens have been considered not explicitly securities so far (though that’s a gray area). The document might state: “ORBT tokens have not been registered under any securities laws. They are not intended for purchase by investors for profit; they are for participation in a decentralized platform. The ORBT DAO does not provide an expectation of profit; any token value is derived from decentralized governance of the protocol.” And so on to cover bases for Howey test.

AML/KYC: ORBT itself (being protocols and code) doesn’t do KYC for regular users. But the foundation or front-end might implement measures like blockchain analytics to flag suspicious addresses (some DApps do that now). For example, if an address interacting with the ORBT web app is flagged as high risk by TRM Labs or Chainalysis (e.g., associated with hacks), the UI might block it. The Terms will inform users that their access to the official web interface can be cut off for compliance reasons, but that doesn’t stop them using the contracts via others or directly.

Jurisdiction of Agreements: If any part of ORBT’s service (like using the official website) constitutes an agreement, it likely falls under a certain jurisdiction’s law. Possibly the foundation’s jurisdiction or something like “governed by the laws of Switzerland” etc. This is included so if there is a dispute, there’s clarity which courts might handle it (though suing a decentralized protocol is novel, the Terms are mostly to disclaim liability).

Tax Implications: A disclaimer might note that using ORBT may have tax consequences (earning yield could be income, trades could be taxable events) and that the project doesn’t provide tax advice. Users should consult their tax advisors
